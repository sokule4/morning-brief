## Cold open

Good morning Sophie, this is your Cyber Brief. Two items lead today. Palo Alto Networks, one of the biggest cybersecurity companies in the world, is reportedly negotiating to buy not one but two young Israeli startups that protect and power AI agents. And Terra Security, an Israeli-founded startup that uses AI to hack companies before real attackers do, just hired its first chief marketing officer, an Israeli marketer from Eon and Lemonade.

## Funding and acquisitions

Let's start with the deals, because this is a big Israeli story.

First, Clutch Security. Clutch was founded in late twenty twenty-three and works in Israel and the US. It protects the keys that AI agents, AI tools that take actions on their own, use to get into a company's systems: passwords, access tokens, API keys, service accounts. In industry language, these are non-human identities, the logins that belong to software rather than people. Clutch maps them, flags ones with too much access, and spots suspicious use. The founders are CEO Ofir Har-Chen, CTO Sagi Haas and Tal Kimhi, who runs research and development.

The Information first reported, and TechCrunch and Calcalist followed, that Palo Alto is in talks to buy Clutch for about five hundred million dollars in cash, plus extra payments if certain milestones are hit. Neither company commented, and the talks could still fall apart. Here's the jaw-dropper. Clutch has raised only about twenty-eight and a half million dollars: an eight and a half million seed round led by Lightspeed in twenty twenty-four, and a twenty million dollar Series A led by SignalFire in January twenty twenty-five, when it was valued at about eighty-five million. TechCrunch says it's nearing ten million dollars in annual recurring revenue, the yearly subscription income a software company can count on. So the price is roughly fifty times its revenue, an enormous multiple even by cyber standards.

Second, Beacon Security, only about two years old. Calcalist reports Palo Alto is also in talks to buy it, with no price disclosed. Beacon lets security teams build and run AI agents that detect, investigate and respond to attacks on their own, and it pulls together data from hundreds of sources so the agents get one clear picture. It says it works with dozens of organizations, including Fortune five hundred companies, and grew its recurring revenue by more than three hundred percent in the first half of this year. Beacon raised a thirteen million dollar seed round in July led by Notable Capital. Its CEO, Gal Tal-Hochberg, previously co-founded HiredScore, which Workday bought for five hundred twenty million dollars, and all three founders are veterans of the Israeli military intelligence units eighty-two hundred and eighty-one.

Why both, and why now? Calcalist's analysis points out that just a month ago, Palo Alto's CEO, Nikesh Arora, said around forty companies were racing to secure AI agents, at least twenty of them Israeli, and that he could afford to wait six to twenty-four months and then buy the winners. His line was, "I have cash. I have market cap. So I can go buy them." Now he's moving within weeks. The two targets sit on opposite sides of the problem: Clutch secures what agents can access, and Beacon uses agents to defend. Clutch would also extend the identity business Palo Alto bought with CyberArk, the Israeli identity security giant, for twenty-five billion dollars. Palo Alto has bought twelve Israeli startups since twenty fourteen, and TechCrunch counts Clutch as what would be its seventh acquisition this year.

The bigger picture for Israel: acquirers are paying for specific capabilities and timing, not for how much a startup has raised. Better-funded Israeli rivals in AI agent security include Zenity, with about one hundred eighty-five million raised, Onyx, Reco and Noma. With about twenty Israeli players chasing the same space, expect more consolidation, meaning startups merging or being bought.

One more giant, with an update on a story we mentioned last week. On Thursday, The Information reported that Cyera, the Israeli data security company, is planning to raise about eight hundred million dollars, led by Cyberstarts, the Israeli venture firm that was one of its earliest backers. The valuation would be twenty billion dollars, up from twelve billion earlier this year. Cyera was founded in twenty twenty-one by Yotam Segev and Tamar Bar-Ilan, and it has already raised more than two billion dollars. Its valuation has roughly doubled every year: three billion, then six, then twelve. These are preliminary talks, so watch for an official announcement.

## People moves

Now people moves, and the one that matters most for you is a first-time marketing chief.

Terra Security named Tal Kapulnik as its first CMO. Terra builds AI agents for offensive security, meaning it attacks a company's systems the way a hacker would, to find holes first, and it does continuous penetration testing, ongoing simulated attacks rather than a once-a-year check. Terra was founded in December twenty twenty-four by CEO Shahar Peled and CTO Gal Malachi, and has raised forty million dollars from investors including Felicis, Dell Technologies Capital and SYN Ventures. Kapulnik's path is worth studying: she was VP, vice president, of marketing at Eon, the cloud backup company, director of organic marketing at Lemonade, the AI insurance company, product marketing for YouTube creators across Europe, the Middle East and Africa at Google, and brand strategy and corporate communications at Edelman, the PR agency. Peled's reason for the hire is a great line for any cyber marketer: security buyers are skeptical of AI claims, and Kapulnik "knows how to build a brand and how to earn the trust of demanding audiences."

Next, Cloudflare, the American company that protects and speeds up a huge share of the internet's websites. It named Christian Smith president of revenue, the executive running its worldwide go-to-market, how a company brings its product to customers, including all of sales. He was CRO at Splunk, the data and security company Cisco bought, where he helped grow annual recurring revenue past four billion dollars, and most recently was an operating partner, a hands-on adviser to portfolio companies, at the investment firm TCV. He replaces Mark Anderson, who retires at the end of the year. The timing is strong: on Thursday Cloudflare reported third-quarter revenue up thirty-nine percent to about seven hundred eighty-two million dollars, raised its forecast, and its shares jumped more than fourteen percent after hours. Its stated goal is five billion dollars in annual recurring revenue.

And a quick security leadership move: Checkmarx, the Israeli-founded application security company, named Kobi Sivilya as CISO, its chief information security officer. He was previously VP of security engineering and IT at Fireblocks and CISO at Outbrain.

## Marketing and growth trends

Now marketing and growth, and today's theme is trust as a selling point.

First, Terra's own words. When a CEO says buyers are skeptical of AI claims and hires a brand builder to fix that, it tells you where cyber marketing is heading. Every vendor now says "AI." The ones that win prove it, with customer stories, transparent results and a voice buyers believe. If you're interviewing, be ready to talk about how you've built credibility with a skeptical technical audience, not just how you generated leads.

Second, the power of owning a category. Clutch is a small company by revenue, yet it may sell for five hundred million dollars, because it became one of the clearest names in a hot new category, securing the identities of AI agents. Beacon made the same move with "agentic security operations," AI agents doing a security team's daily work. Clear category language, repeated everywhere, makes a startup easy for both buyers and acquirers to find. That's a positioning job, and it's marketing's job.

Third, look at how OpenAI is launching GPT-6 Cyber, its new model for finding security holes. It's going first to a limited group of vetted customers through a program called Daybreak Red, and OpenAI rates its cyber capabilities as "Critical." In security, a controlled, invitation-only launch builds trust and exclusivity at the same time, and it's a pattern worth borrowing for any sensitive product.

## Worth a look for you

Finally, worth a look for you. First, Terra Security. A brand-new first CMO at a forty million dollar, Israeli-founded company almost always means building a team: product marketing, the people who shape how a product is pitched, content, events and demand generation, the marketers who fill the sales pipeline. Early is the best time to reach out.

Second, the other Israeli AI agent security startups. If Palo Alto buys Clutch and Beacon, rivals like Zenity, Onyx, Reco and Noma will need sharper positioning to stand apart, and fast. That's a moment when companies invest in marketing.

Third, Cyera. If an eight hundred million dollar round closes, a company that size keeps hiring across marketing, from brand to field marketing, meaning events and local teams, and partner marketing, campaigns with resellers, and it has a big Tel Aviv presence.

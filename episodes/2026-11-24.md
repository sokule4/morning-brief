## Cold open

Good morning Sophie. It's Tuesday, November twenty-fourth, and this is your Morning Brief.

Here are today's three biggest stories. CrowdStrike agreed to buy Zscaler for about forty-four billion dollars, the biggest cybersecurity deal ever, and it brings nine Israeli startups under one roof. Amazon confirmed it's buying Decart, the young Israeli AI company, in a deal reported at seven and a half billion dollars. And the Bank of Israel kept interest rates where they are, because the shekel is falling so fast.

## Israel

We start here at home, where the coalition puzzle got a new piece on Monday, and it came from Naftali Bennett. Speaking to soldiers visiting the Knesset, Bennett, who leads the B'Yachad party, laid out three possible governments. One that relies on the Arab parties. One that relies on the ultra-Orthodox parties. And a third that he clearly prefers: a unity government with Likud, built on a rotation agreement, where two leaders take turns as prime minister, with Gadi Eisenkot going first. He didn't say who would take the second turn. "People who can serve together in a tank can also run the country together," he said. And then, more honestly: "I don't know if we'll succeed."

Why does this matter? Because Bennett is part of Eisenkot's camp, and the rest of that camp has promised voters it won't sit with Netanyahu at all. A rotation with Likud would mean exactly that, sharing power with Netanyahu's party. That's why the reaction inside the bloc was quick. Yair Golan, who leads the Democrats, the left-wing party, said: "It's time to stop the sham talks with Likud, and form a government." He also urged Bennett publicly to reject any unity deal with Netanyahu. And Times of Israel reports that Eisenkot and Bennett are split over whether to give Likud a deadline in the talks. Remember, Bennett also held a two-hour meeting with Netanyahu last week without telling his partners, so his allies are watching him closely.

Eisenkot, meanwhile, seems to be planning a surprise. According to Kan, the public broadcaster, as reported by Times of Israel, Eisenkot intends to announce, at a moment of his choosing in the coming weeks, that he has a coalition, and then call a Knesset vote within a day or two. The idea is to leave his opponents no time to pull partners away. That government could be a minority government, one that survives because some parties abstain rather than vote against it. The catch, as Times of Israel points out, is that each of his options has been publicly ruled out by someone in his own bloc.

And one more twist from overnight. Channel 13 reports that Eisenkot met secretly on Sunday with David Zini, the head of the Shin Bet, about the investigation into alleged vote-buying in the Arab community during the election. Quick background: last week the Central Elections Committee said it found no evidence of bribery on a scale that could have changed the results. Now, according to the report, Zini is distancing himself from that announcement, while the judge who chairs the committee, Deputy Supreme Court President Noam Sohlberg, stressed that Zini signed off on it. People around Eisenkot told Channel 13 that Zini is a "political Shin Bet head" serving Netanyahu's interests. Why it matters: if the head of the security service casts doubt on the election's cleanliness, Netanyahu's camp gets fuel to question the results, and that makes any new government's legitimacy harder to establish.

What's next: Eisenkot's twenty-eight days run out around December sixth, and the president can add fourteen more.

Now the economy, and your wallet. On Monday, the Bank of Israel, led by Governor Amir Yaron, kept its interest rate at three point two five percent, the second meeting in a row with no change, after three cuts earlier this year. Normally, with inflation at just one point one percent in October, below the middle of the bank's target range, you'd expect another cut. So why not? The shekel. According to Globes, it has fallen about ten percent against the dollar in less than a month, and about twenty percent since the end of May. A Bank Hapoalim strategist called it "one of the three weakest currencies in the world" since the election. Only Brazil's and Colombia's currencies did worse.

There are a few reasons. Investors see a higher risk premium, the extra return they demand for holding something risky, because of the political deadlock and the chance of yet another election. The gap between Israeli and American interest rates is widening, which makes the dollar more attractive. And Israeli pension and investment funds, which hold huge portfolios abroad, are selling shekels to rebalance their hedging, the protection they buy against currency swings. For you, a weaker shekel means imported goods, flights abroad and anything priced in dollars get more expensive. The bank said "uncertainty remains high," and markets now give about a forty-three percent chance of a cut in one of the next two decisions.

On security and society, President Isaac Herzog called the weekend's settler arson attacks near Ramallah "despicable" and an "assault on God himself." And David Barnea, the former head of the Mossad, said Israel must go back to war against Iran "as soon as all conditions allow." The army says there are no changes to the public's safety guidelines for now.

## World

Now to the world, and the Iran war. Iran's foreign ministry spokesman, Esmaeil Baghaei, said Monday that Iran "will use all available means to counter US aggression." He was talking about America's naval blockade, which has kept Iran's oil exports near zero. He called the blockade "an act of military aggression" and pointed to Article fifty-one of the UN Charter, the part that gives countries the right to self-defense. That's legal language, but it's also a warning: Iran is building the case that anything it does next is self-defense.

He also offered a way out, on Iran's terms. Iran could reopen the Strait of Hormuz "within two days," he said, if Washington stops the hostilities, lifts the blockade and waives sanctions. Then, overnight, Iran's Revolutionary Guard said three oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines, explosives hidden in the water, in the strait. That's Iran's own claim, and there are no details yet on the ships or the crews. An Al Jazeera correspondent in Tehran summed up the mood: "Escalation is now the main option on the table." And Qatar's prime minister, whose country is mediating, told Al Jazeera the region cannot handle another round between America and Iran.

President Trump, for his part, wrote on Truth Social that Iran was emboldened by the Democrats' midterm win, claiming it "has launched more drones and missiles in the last week than at any time for over a year," and that all of them were knocked down. He also cast doubt on the election results, saying, without evidence, that "the whole election was very strange."

Speaking of the midterms, the final count is still coming in, and it keeps getting worse for Republicans. In North Carolina, Democrat Chuck Hubbard beat Virginia Foxx, a Republican who had served eleven terms in the House. A recount, a second official count when the result is very close, confirmed he won by just three hundred thirty votes out of about two hundred eighty-seven thousand. Foxx conceded. Democrats now have their biggest House majority in about three decades.

In the war in Ukraine, President Zelenskyy confirmed on Monday that Ukraine was behind an attack on a Russian warship in a port in Libya, in North Africa, far from the front lines. Libya's eastern government said it looked like an unmanned vehicle did it. The Kremlin called it "terrorist activity." Why it matters: Ukraine is showing it can hit Russia's navy almost anywhere, and it comes a few days after Russian missiles fell inside Poland, and NATO's top body was set to meet in Brussels on Monday.

And on climate, the UN's COP thirty-one summit, the yearly Conference of the Parties, in Antalya, Turkey, ended without a new deal. It ran past its Sunday deadline, making it the longest UN climate conference ever. The European Union and Pacific island countries objected that the final text didn't mention fossil fuels at all, and other countries complained there were no clear money commitments for poorer nations.

## New York

## Tech, startups and AI

Next, tech, and it's official: one of the biggest Israeli AI exits ever. On Monday, Amazon confirmed it's buying Decart. We told you on Saturday the talks were close; now there's a signed deal. Amazon didn't give a price, but Bloomberg and the Israeli press put it at about seven and a half billion dollars.

What does Decart do? It's famous for AI that generates video and interactive worlds in real time. But what Amazon wants is the engine underneath: software that makes big AI models run efficiently on different kinds of chips, from Nvidia, Google and Amazon. That's the key. Amazon makes its own AI chips, called Trainium, and wants to sell them as an alternative to Nvidia's. Decart's software could make it easier for companies to switch. Amazon's Peter DeSantis put it simply: "Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip."

The founders are a story in themselves. Brothers Dean and Orian Leitersdorf and Moshe Shalev met in Unit 8200, the army's intelligence tech unit. Dean finished a PhD at the Technion at twenty-three. The company was founded only at the end of twenty twenty-three, has about one hundred twenty people, and was valued at about four billion dollars in May. Globes reports the deal is paid in shares tied to targets over five years, the founders must stay five years, and Decart joins Annapurna Labs, Amazon's chip division, which itself was an Israeli startup Amazon bought ten years ago.

## Business story

Our business story is about an Israeli real estate empire shrinking back home. G City, the company once called Gazit Globe, has owned shopping centers and commercial property across the world, from America to Scandinavia. On an investor call Monday, its founder and controlling shareholder, the person whose stake gives them control, Chaim Katzman, who built the company over thirty-five years, said: "We have decided to sell all of our holdings in the United States." That's about three hundred fifty million dollars in American assets, and he says talks are "quite advanced."

Why? To cut leverage, the amount of borrowed money the company runs on. G City has big debts coming due in twenty twenty-seven and twenty twenty-eight, and a deal to sell control of the company to another businessman recently collapsed. Its Scandinavian arm, Citycon, is also selling about eight hundred fifty million euros of property this year. From now on, Katzman says, the focus is Israel and Poland. It's a reminder that in real estate, the big story right now isn't growth. It's who has too much debt when interest rates stay high.

## Travel

## Wellness

And finally, wellness, and a tip if you're not sleeping well. Researchers at Harbin Sport University in China did a meta-analysis, a study that combines many earlier studies, of thirty trials with more than twenty-five hundred adults who had sleep problems, published in the journal Sleep and Biological Rhythms. The winner was yoga, and specifically a more intense style of yoga. The best result came from just two sessions a week, up to thirty minutes each. Walking came second, then strength training. The researchers say more high-quality studies are needed, but the practical takeaway is easy: two short yoga classes a week might do more for your sleep than you'd expect.

## Part 2: Cyber Watch

## Funding and acquisitions

It finally happened. On Monday, CrowdStrike and Zscaler announced that CrowdStrike will buy Zscaler in an all-stock deal worth about forty-four billion dollars. It's the biggest acquisition in cybersecurity history, well above Google's thirty-two billion dollars for Wiz.

Quick refresher. CrowdStrike, based in Texas, is known for protecting endpoints, the laptops and servers people use. Zscaler, from California, is known for zero trust network security in the cloud, checking every connection a user makes before letting it through. CrowdStrike's revenue last quarter was about one point six billion dollars; Zscaler's was about nine hundred fifty million.

The terms: Zscaler shareholders get one point one three five CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share. That's an exchange ratio, a fixed number of buyer shares per target share. Based on Friday's prices, it values Zscaler at about two hundred sixty dollars a share, a premium, meaning the price above the current share price, of about thirty-four percent. CrowdStrike's shareholders will own about eighty-four percent of the combined company. Jay Chaudhry, Zscaler's founder and CEO, will keep running the Zscaler business and join CrowdStrike's board, and he and his affiliates, who hold about a third of Zscaler's shares, have already signed agreements to vote yes. The deal needs approval from regulators and Zscaler's shareholders, and is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's twenty twenty-eight fiscal year, the company's own twelve-month accounting year.

TheStreet points out the catch: because the ratio is fixed, if CrowdStrike's stock falls, Zscaler holders get less. And it did fall, by about five to six percent before the market opened, while Zscaler jumped by roughly thirteen to twenty percent, depending on the report. Reuters says analysts see possible antitrust concerns.

Now the Israeli angle, from Calcalist. This deal brings nine Israeli cyber companies under one roof. CrowdStrike bought six Israeli startups since twenty twenty, including Bionic, Flow Security, Adaptive Shield and Seraphic. Zscaler bought three, including Avalor for about three hundred fifty million dollars. Together they have more than seven hundred employees here, about five hundred at CrowdStrike and around two hundred at Zscaler, both with offices in Ramat Gan's Diamond Exchange district. Neither company has announced changes to its Israeli teams.

One more new round, and it has Israeli roots. Q.security came out of stealth on Monday with an eleven and a half million dollar seed round led by Castle Island Ventures, with Coinbase Ventures, New York Life Ventures and Ground Up Ventures joining. It helps big companies move to post-quantum cryptography, encryption that quantum computers can't break. The founders, CEO Guy Peer and Nir Steinherz, both came from Unbound Security, the Israeli encryption startup Coinbase bought in twenty twenty-one. Eleven and a half million is a healthy seed round, but not a giant one. It says the market for quantum-safe security is real but still early.

## Marketing and growth trends

Now marketing and growth, and this deal is a masterclass in how to tell a merger story.

First, the one-line narrative. George Kurtz, CrowdStrike's founder and CEO, said: "The network used to connect people to places. Now it connects agents to data." In one sentence, he takes a deal that could sound like a giant buying a rival and turns it into a story about the future, AI agents. Chaudhry used the same frame: "AI agents are rapidly becoming the new weakest link." Notice both CEOs are telling the same story. That's not an accident; it's coordinated messaging.

Second, getting ahead of the objections. Daniel Bernard, CrowdStrike's chief business officer, told CyberScoop, "this isn't a shotgun wedding," and stressed that customers can still plug in other vendors' tools. Why say that? Because the biggest fear buyers have about platformization is getting locked into one vendor. He named the fear before customers could. And he gave the ambition a memorable phrase: "We want to be the operating system of security."

Third, the selling plan. The companies say about eight thousand Zscaler customers don't buy from CrowdStrike today, and they want to sell both product sets under a single contract. They're targeting about a billion dollars a year in revenue synergies, extra sales the merged company expects to make together, within thirty months. For marketers, that means cross-sell campaigns, joint packaging and one story for two very different buyers: the endpoint team and the network team.

And a small lesson from Q.security. Its CEO's line, "quantum computing is making cryptographic risk an urgent and ongoing business priority," is all about urgency. Selling something whose threat is years away is hard, so the message turns a future risk into a to-do list for today. And it plans to sell both directly and through channel partners from the start.

## Worth a look for you

Finally, worth a look for you.

First, CrowdStrike and Zscaler in Israel. More than seven hundred people across nine acquired companies, going through what will be a long integration. Integrations need product marketers and internal communicators who can explain one combined story. Watch for roles in Ramat Gan over the next year.

Second, Q.security. A fresh seed round, founders with Israeli startup roots, and a category that needs explaining. Companies like this usually hire their first marketer to build the story and the first campaigns. Worth checking where its team is based.

Third, the companies Zscaler and CrowdStrike compete with, like Palo Alto Networks and Israel's Cato Networks. When the biggest players merge, rivals respond with sharper positioning and new campaigns aimed at nervous customers. That's often when they hire.
